# One contextual agent across customers, independent sellers and teams

**Date:** 2026-10-28. **Status:** independent design/review proposal against `ab1a0a6`; not implementation, verification, or approval of new autonomous actions. **Ownership:** this report only. Existing specs, prototypes and code were not changed. No archive directory was read. No browser screenshots, live deployments, external provider compatibility or new test results are claimed.

## Parent synthesis after the review (2026-10-28)

Mouhamed clarified the framing while this review ran: **operating model**, rather than “rules,” is the product/domain term. It defines RBAC, ACL and the business behavior it covers; those are facets of the operating model, not independent authorization systems bolted onto the agent. The target is applied AI harness engineering: one contextual agent whose environment, tools and permitted effects are derived from that operating model. The existing implementation has fixed roles and guards outside `engine-rules`; extending their common evaluation is planned work, not a claim that dynamic ACL already ships in the package.

Modern harness execution policies are useful inspiration: supervised, accept routine edits, auto within limits, and full permitted access. They describe how independently the agent uses existing authority. “Full access” cannot grant a new role, bypass a record relationship or remove a mandatory human/payment gate. Product labels and allowed effects must be expressed by business use case before those policies are enabled; the developer vocabulary is not the customer UI.

Use mainstream words on screen: **My purchases, Requests, My work, Projects, Products, Orders, Earnings, Payments, Approvals, Team, Operating model**. Explain “Needs your approval,” “Waiting for the client,” “Scheduled,” or “Unavailable because …”; keep harness, state-machine, RBAC, ACL, principal and policy-version terminology in engineering documentation. Agency/studio are customer business types; AG Studio remains the cancelled analytics integration.

The [parent prototype pass and verification](../hackathon/dashboard-prototype-notes.md) records subsequent fixes; the prototype observations below describe the pre-pass revision. The recommendations below remain an independent proposal. The parent is adopting the reversible design/fixture corrections and recording source-level implementation prerequisites. New schema, real team-policy changes and autonomous effects are not enabled by this report.

## 1. Verdict and recommended direction

Keep **one Payleor agent, the existing business records and their state machines, and one authoritative action path**. Change its authorized environment, presentation and available tools with the person's current capacity and the record's facts. Do not create a customer agent, consultant agent, merchant agent, agency agent and studio agent. Equally, do not turn every person into a services-team administrator with different navigation labels.

The current direction fits agency operations well: incoming work, assignment, delivery, money, member payouts and exceptions. Its foundations also fit independent sellers and customers. The draft fails where it makes the account/workspace tree an access model, makes attention handles permission roles, and treats a payout-oriented staff Call as the default experience for everyone. The key composition is:

> Product capabilities determine which jobs exist. Current memberships and explicit grants determine authority. Relationships determine which records and fields that authority covers. Business state and pinned terms determine which transitions are valid. Automation policy determines who may carry out an already-authorized transition. Context determines relevance; a handle determines attention. Neither grants access.

Agency and studio are useful onboarding language and rule presets, not separate authorization classes. The site's own definition of studios is businesses selling services and products, including salons and pottery studios ([site design, lines 13–18](2026-10-19-site-and-design-system-design.md#L13)). A studio can therefore compose services and store capabilities. Appointment calendars, chairs, inventory fulfilment and multi-worker project allocation are not implied by the word “studio.” Do not invent those transitions for this delivery. “AG Studio cancelled” concerns the analytics integration, not studio customers.

Recommend six immediate design corrections:

1. Preserve workspace/organization and seller/team as distinct identifiers. A workspace can contain several sellers; a record remains owned by its seller. A UI context is not a replacement foreign key.
2. Put **My purchases** in the mandatory personal workspace as a private view of externally owned records. Keep account home as a compact overview of authorized contexts. Do not copy purchases into the person's seller records or grant their personal-workspace collaborators access.
3. Keep fixed roles for the first implementation, behind a permission evaluator. Handles initially route to these roles or record relationships. Editable permissions can follow without rebuilding Calls.
4. Build an authorized record projection and an eligibility/required-decision evaluator before offering model tools. Existing status-only helpers and broad record responses are insufficient.
5. Present one automation settings surface with explicit executors and effects; retain existing deterministic schedules until an explicit migration. Payleor mode is not an undocumented platform-wide stop switch.
6. Start with scoped reads, human-confirmed intake and payout decisions, and honest deterministic payout outcomes. Add discretionary Auto only after the delegation and action-specific limits below are implemented and verified.

These recommendations are concrete defaults for the parent to incorporate into the next design/plan. They do not authorize schema migration, deployment, changing team policies, or enabling autonomous financial actions.

## 2. Use cases, objects and relationships

“Customer,” “consultant,” “merchant,” “agency” and “studio” describe jobs or business arrangements. The same account may occupy several rows simultaneously. A role is always attached to a workspace/seller; a client or payee relationship is attached to the relevant record or obligation.

### Four scenarios that should drive the design

- **Aicha:** owns a solo consultancy, bids in Walk Crew, buys a logo from another seller. In her solo context she runs projects and sees incoming money; in Walk Crew she sees eligible requests and her work; in My purchases she accepts the external logo. None of these grants crosses to the others.
- **Mouhamed:** workspace admin with several seller teams. Today that role inherits team admin inside that organization. Keep that documented inheritance, not a global account-owner override. Each financial action still names one seller/payee and enforces self-approval restrictions.
- **Maison Lune:** team selling a service and a download. Enable both jobs without turning a download into an assigned project or inventing appointment scheduling. “Studio” seeds a suitable setup, not a permission bypass.
- **Two payout approvers:** first approval completes that person's required decision, not the second approver's. The first sees “Waiting for another approver”; the second sees Needs you; both see one business payout and the same remaining approval facts.

## 3. Actor, role, relationship and context model

### 3.1 Vocabulary and ownership

| Term | Recommended meaning |
|---|---|
| Account | A person and their authenticated identities; root of their navigation, not the owner of every workspace's data |
| Personal workspace | Mandatory private place for personal settings, purchases and own earnings views; seller capability can be enabled according to subscription. Private purchases do not become shareable just because selling is enabled |
| Workspace | Existing organization and its memberships; can contain several seller teams/stores |
| Seller | Existing team/site/merchant owner of service or order records; keep `workspaceId` and `sellerId/teamId` distinct |
| Product capabilities | Services, store, collaborative delivery, member payouts, etc., enabled by supported product/plan settings. Entitlement permits a feature to exist, not a person to execute it |
| Role | Named permission bundle assigned within a workspace/seller. Initially fixed admin/manager/freelancer plus existing workspace inheritance |
| Relationship | Attached verified client, assigned worker, quote author, manager, payee, request audience/shortlist, order-grant holder; constrains records, fields and conflicts of interest |
| Permission | Stable business ability such as quote for team or approve payout, independent of a role/handle's display name |
| Handle | Address for routing attention: a person, permission-role audience, group or resolved relationship. Membership in a notification group supplies no authority |
| Context | Navigation/retrieval envelope: account → workspace → seller when needed → section → record, with a bounded set of explicitly brought-in references |
| Call | A durable place for a decision episode or a person's question, referencing authoritative records; its triage is derived, not a new project/payment status |
| Delegation | Revocable, bounded authority for the runtime to perform specified work for a named human principal, inside one seller and current policy |

Today a seller is a Better Auth team in an organization; workspace owners/admins inherit access to its sellers ([architecture, lines 74–79](2026-10-16-architecture.md#L74), [effectiveServicesTeamRole, lines 52–58](../../packages/core/src/services-teams/permissions.ts#L52)). Do not implement the draft's “workspace is a team” shorthand as a destructive merge of those identities. The subscription/workspace-type direction should expose supported capabilities through this structure, not change project ownership or convert a marketing label into an authorization role. Subscription billing is not yet built ([architecture, lines 88–90, 122–123](2026-10-16-architecture.md#L88)).

### 3.2 Personal purchases: recommended placement and migration

Show purchases under **Personal › My purchases**, reachable from account home. The seller retains each project, contact, order and ledger; the personal view resolves the person's verified relationships on demand. Existing `/me/projects` can become this view without moving business rows. Keep no-account project/order links working with their original narrow authority. A forwarded pay link must not yield review/acceptance rights; the dated security audit identifies precisely that failure and its A2 remedy ([security audit, lines 46–50, 98](2026-10-16-security-audit.md#L46)).

Do not auto-claim guest store orders by matching the checkout email to an account. Store orders currently have `buyerEmail` and download grants, not an account/customer relationship ([orders schema, lines 14–41, 49–62](../../packages/db/src/schema/orders.ts#L14)). A signed-in order history therefore needs a verified claim/linking flow or remains a link-only purchase in this slice. A typed email is insufficient. Client projects already have the stronger A2 contact model; reuse it rather than a global cross-seller contact merge.

For rollout, create the missing private personal container idempotently, preserve existing organizations and solo sellers, and map navigation first. Do not reparent existing seller teams merely to make the switcher prettier. If personal seller collaboration becomes possible later, personal purchases and earnings retain user-private ACLs independent of workspace admin access.

### 3.3 Evaluation contract: same answer for browsing, widgets and execution

Implement one deep **Business environment module** at the application seam, composed internally from the existing tenancy, client resolution, audience rules, state machine, operating rules and payout evaluators. Its small external interface is conceptually:

```text
inspect(actor, context, recordRefs, purpose)
  -> authorized projections, eligible actions, required decisions,
     external/timer waits, policy/version references, explanation facts

prepare(actor-or-delegation, action, exact inputs)
  -> durable proposal with effect preview, source references and preconditions

perform(authenticated actor, action, exact inputs, proposal/confirmation?, idempotencyKey)
  -> committed result or current refusal; then inspect again
```

Names are illustrative; extend existing `perform`, do not wrap it with an unchecked alternative executor. `inspect` is advisory and cannot mint an enduring permission. Share rule evaluators with execution, loading current facts again at the transaction/side-effect claim. The model gets focused tools generated from this result, not arbitrary database queries or a catch-all execute tool. The module earns its depth by hiding these rules from every screen/adapter/model caller.

Order of evaluation:

1. Authenticate the actual requester; resolve the record's owning seller and workspace from trusted storage. Validate the host/seller/record combination. Never trust model-supplied role, team or relationship.
2. Resolve **current** workspace and seller membership, or narrow client/order relationship. `resolveClient` must still refuse a same-team member, verify attachment/link version and resolve verified account identities ([clients.ts, lines 41–81](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/clients.ts#L41)). Re-run this on execute/resume; an old resolved `client_account` object is not a permanent grant.
3. Apply product capability, role permission and record/field/space ACL. Explicit prohibition wins; a role grant cannot bypass a self-approval rule, client exclusion, department/shortlist restriction or confidential field rule. Multiple roles combine permissions **within the same seller only**, with these prohibitions retained.
4. Build permitted fields before sending anything to the model. Masking, rival quote exclusion, customer-only views, own-earnings restrictions, files and aggregate counts apply at retrieval. Never retrieve with an admin and ask the model to forget restricted fields.
5. Evaluate current business status plus full facts: pinned rulebook, pending amendment/top-up, acceptance, released earnings/reservations, dispute holds, recipient, amount/currency, required distinct approvals and provider uncertainty.
6. Evaluate execution channel, action's hard agent policy, human confirmation requirements, current delegation and current automation policy. More Auto cannot enlarge any earlier result.
7. Produce two different projections: **all permitted optional actions** and **required decisions/waits/outcome**. Sending an optional reminder neither completes the customer's review nor creates an approval obligation.
8. At execution, bind consent to exact action, subject, beneficiary, amounts/currency and material versions. Recheck all relevant facts and commit decision, durable intent and outbox together. Refresh from authoritative results, not the model's assertion that it succeeded.

Use coarse status metadata only as a candidate filter. The current `allowedProjectEvents` accepts optional partial facts with defaults ([state-machine.ts, lines 117–134](../../packages/core/src/projects/state-machine.ts#L117)); do not call it with missing facts and label the result fully eligible.

### 3.4 Handles and roles: preserve the useful simplicity

Replace “handles route attention and permissions” ([calls design, lines 35–41](2026-10-27-calls-and-agent-design.md#L35)) with: **handles address people; grants authorize actions**. A role handle may reference a permission role; it does not infer permissions from its name. A person can belong to several attention groups without receiving combined administrative authority.

For the first slice seed `@sales` to eligible quoting staff, `@payouts` to current payout approvers and `@admins` to current admins. Resolve `@assignee` from assignment, and `@lead` from an explicitly defined manager/lead relationship; do not silently equate a freelancer, manager and lead. A solo owner's staff handles may route to the owner, but no payout-approval permission appears when the solo capability has no member payouts. A sole team admin still cannot approve their own payout.

Later, editable permission roles should be separate records from attention groups, even if one settings screen edits them together. Require authority to grant the specific permission, preserve protected owner recovery, log changes, and bump an authorization revision. Renaming a handle changes no grant. Deleting a routing group cannot erase a required business decision: route to an authorized fallback, or show an administrative configuration problem with no bypass.

### 3.5 Context composition and shared Calls

- **Account overview:** allowlisted summary projections for each authorized context, computed under that person's rights. A worker's summary contains their work and earnings, not team revenue. Customer projects across sellers are visible through their client relationships. No account-level financial mutations or cross-seller bulk approval.
- **One link away:** permission-filter every linked record and field. A project-to-member link may return display name and role, not destination credentials or all that person's earnings. Search results, autocomplete, badges and chart cells obey the same rule; counts must not reveal hidden populations.
- **Bring in:** expand relevance only after checking access. Same-workspace records can change the displayed context to workspace; two workspaces change it to account. Acting still resolves one owning seller/record per action. Bringing a foreign supplier into a quote must not silently send supplier details to the consultant's client.
- **Cross-workspace summaries:** private, explicitly requested comparison of permitted summary fields, labelled by source, date range and currency. Do not persist combined prose into either workspace's shared Call. Detailed operations require entering the relevant context; mixed-context tool inputs cannot pool grants or balances.
- **Shared Calls:** only share a Call among an audience entitled to every item and derived entry it contains. For v1, keep team Calls seller-local and audience-homogeneous; keep broader and mixed-capacity questions private. Bringing in sensitive context narrows the audience or starts a private question with a link back; it must not widen who can read it. Check both the person's access and the Call audience before preparing a shared answer.
- **Customer and seller Calls:** they can reference the same required decision but use separate audience views. A seller-private payout explanation is not the customer's conversation. Explicit **Send to client** publishes chosen wording through the business conversation action, with the audience shown; private agent chat is never automatically relayed.
- **Existing business conversation history:** the approved two-space design gives the accepted worker the project's full history, including relayed request activity ([conversations design, lines 15–19, 32–44](2026-10-14-conversations-design.md#L15)). “Team only” before acceptance therefore must not be represented as permanent internal confidentiality. Private agent Calls are a separate audience contract. If persistent staff-only notes are introduced later, give them an explicit ACL; do not claim they already exist in the project space or silently hide agreed project history from the accepted worker.
- **Resume:** rebuild from currently authorized sources before loading historical text into the model. Remove inaccessible attachments and derived summaries; an unsanitizable mixed transcript is sealed and a fresh authorized continuation begins. Revoking a source does not make its earlier copied summary safe. Already viewed information cannot be retroactively undisclosed; prevent further retrieval and execution.

The proposed Call schema's mandatory `team_id` and single `handle_id` cannot by themselves represent personal/account questions, audience revisions or permission-safe derived entries ([calls design, lines 99–105](2026-10-27-calls-and-agent-design.md#L99)). Keep one seller-owned decision episode with addressed recipients; resolve ACL separately. Add a private user-owned question form with context references. Store owner, audience policy, record refs, outcome predicate and provenance—not a persisted authoritative `waiting_on` business state. Handle changes must not duplicate the same financial obligation into two independent Calls.

## 5. Automation, delegation, approvals and versioning

### 5.1 One policy surface, explicit execution mechanisms

Keep Manual / Ask me / Auto as the amount of **discretion entrusted to Payleor**, not a universal label for every background mechanism. Put all related behavior in one **Automation** settings surface per seller/section, grouped by business effect: asking/quoting, reviewing by timeout, approving/sending payouts, notices and reconciliation. Show executor, source/version, limits, required approvals, timing and whether it is enabled. A read-only summary chip in the header links there; the exact placement is a recommended UX decision, not a claim of user approval.

| Mode | New discretionary preparation | New discretionary action | Existing deterministic work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manual | No automatic drafts; read questions still work. Explicit “Draft this” is an on-demand request with no external effect | Human acts in normal form; nothing automatic because of a chat instruction | Continues under its disclosed accepted/configured rule |
| Ask me (default) | Prepare only authorized work; persist source/version/effect preview | Authorized human confirms exact proposal; sending a draft is not a read | Same; show e.g. “Payleor prepares; approved payouts still send on payday” |
| Auto | Same preparation, subject to budget and event/run deduplication | Only explicitly allowlisted effects within current grant, state and limits; otherwise a human decision | Same authoritative deterministic path; agent must not create a competing send/reminder |

This is a coherent final model if “mode” always names Payleor's discretion and automation settings enumerate all background effects. It is incoherent if a prominent Ask me badge is the only explanation while payouts send. Do not preserve that UX ambiguity merely with a footnote in documentation.

Implement a read-only compatibility projection of existing payout rules/schedules first. Then use a versioned operational policy for new discretion. Do not rewrite accepted project terms when someone changes drafting mode. A later migration may let a person change or pause future scheduled sends in the same settings transaction, after showing affected items. It must distinguish new sends from irrevocable in-flight sends and reconciliation. This review does **not** propose that switching to Manual silently pauses the engine.

Recommended initial allowlist: authorized reads, explanation and preparation; human-confirmed seller questions/quotes and payout approvals. Optional first discretionary Auto action: sending narrowly templated missing-information questions with recipient, volume and audience limits. Keep quotes human-confirmed until an explicit commercial pricing envelope is chosen. “Within 10% of similar work” is neither a reliable price floor nor a permission; it is an old proposal ([contextual reference, lines 210–227](2026-10-18-contextual-dash-ontology-design.md#L210)). Payout Auto in the first demo should use the existing explicit deterministic rules, accurately attributed. Demonstrate agent-originated autonomous execution only when its actual principal and policy path have evidence.

Hard gates are independent of mode: customer quote/delivery/amendment commitments stay human; checkout approval stays with the payer; refunds, reduction/waiver/warning actions currently marked `never`, role/settings/connection changes and case responses remain on their permitted human path. `free` does not mean anonymous or unconstrained. `confirm` must not be reinterpreted globally as “Auto can bypass confirmation”; define an action-specific eligible delegation path and preserve locked human requirements.

### 5.2 The actual unattended principal

Recommend **a named runtime identity plus a persisted delegation from one current authorized human per seller**, not a handle, team password, model session, outbox event or public API key. This preserves the action catalog's intended one-level human `onBehalfOf` model while supporting unattended runs ([action catalog, lines 77–87, 116–121](2026-10-13-action-catalog-design.md#L77)).

Proposed grant facts: `id`, seller/workspace, runtime identity, delegating user, allowed action names, permitted record selector/audience, limits by currency/action/period, policy revision, expiry, revocation time and current authorization revision. Server evaluates selectors; they are not model-written SQL. A request-bound customer delegation may read only the customer's verified records and prepare; it never gains seller permissions or unattended customer acceptance. No delegation chain.

Interactive reads/preparation use that viewer's authority. Event-driven preparation uses the narrow persisted grant and the intended Call audience, not an arbitrary admin chosen by handle membership. On grant issuance, verify the issuer can delegate each permission; at prepare, execute and resume require the issuer still has it. Removal, downgrade, expiry or revocation stops **future new delegated effects** and unsent proposals. Another admin can explicitly adopt/reissue the delegation; no silent fallback to whichever admin remains. Emit a configuration/authority exception to authorized administrators without leaking the stopped run's content.

Human confirmation executes as the authenticated human with durable proposal/agent attribution. Unattended execution records runtime identity, delegation id and human sponsor; it never masquerades as the human having clicked. Provider credentials remain inside the server's existing payout/payment adapter. The runtime gets a narrow internal authenticated transport to `perform`, never direct database or PayPal credentials. B2 external seller keys and webhook delivery remain separate work.

### 5.3 Precedence and version contract

Use **three references**, not one all-powerful pinned “Calls” rule:

1. **Pinned business contract** for each project: rates/splits, revisions, response and silence rules, accepted terms. Changes to routing or agent preference cannot rewrite it. Stored payment rate/amount and recipient snapshots remain authoritative.
2. **Current access and delegation**: membership, permission bundles, record/field/space grants, conflict rules, revocation. A pinned old rulebook cannot resurrect a removed worker or approver.
3. **Current operational automation policy**: who may prepare/execute which effects, limits, routing and current payout-send configuration. Version it separately from project terms, even if edited in a nearby rules UI. Business rulebook summaries may link to it; do not make every mode change require customers to reaccept contractual terms.

Before a fresh effect, apply the intersection of pinned business constraints and current access/delegation/operational safety gates. Current hard holds and explicit deny always win. A policy relaxation does not silently upgrade an old proposal; regenerate and, where needed, reconfirm it. Tightening invalidates outstanding discretionary proposals. A policy change must not silently resize a customer's accepted payment or a member's already approved recipient/amount.

Existing payouts use current team payout settings rather than pinned project books ([payouts.ts, lines 535–547](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/payouts.ts#L535), [payout sweep, lines 20–36](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/payout-sweeps.ts#L20)). Preserve that behavior initially, with an explicit policy revision and change-impact preview. Once a payout's decision is recorded, freeze its amount/beneficiary and required approval policy for that attempt; any material mutation starts a fresh decision. If current policy becomes more restrictive before send, hold for the stronger requirement; relaxing policy does not retroactively count missing approvals. This is a proposed enhancement, not a description of today's schema.

### 5.4 Multi-approval, batches and amounts

- A batching period groups attention. It does not merge debts, providers, currencies, pinned contracts or permission grants. Start with one seller, one currency and one compatible send/approval policy per actionable group. Do not introduce the proposed Friday 17:00 timezone behavior where today's persisted payday is 00:00 UTC ([OUT-20](../stories/07-payouts.md#L263)). Show actual time now; design timezone scheduling separately.
- A member-request payout may draw on released earnings from several projects and have no single `projectId`. If a guard needs source-project contracts, resolve the reserved ledger allocations server-side; never pretend the selected display project alone proves its eligibility. An automatic project payout has a different source and hold rule ([OUT-07, OUT-17/18](../stories/07-payouts.md#L94)).
- Evaluate each selected payout against its own records/contracts, then current seller policy. Show group breakdowns and each rule version; do not pick the newest project rulebook as the batch's contract. If provenance cannot be resolved, exclude that item with the current reason.
- Required approvals are distinct authorized humans, excluding payee; never count the agent, the same human twice or aliases/handles as different approvers. Revalidate approval authority before a fresh send under the new contract. Preserve approval history if a person loses authority, but hold/recollect any now-insufficient approvals. This adds protection beyond today's stored approver-id count and requires coverage.
- Freeze the reviewed selection by ids/revisions/effect digest. The confirmation says exactly how many rows, how much, which currency, who is paid, what is approved and when sending may occur. A newly eligible row does not silently join an approved amount. A new request can join the Call while remaining outside the frozen proposal.
- Batch preparation can report eligible/held items. Confirmation of that displayed subset is deliberate; if eligibility materially changes before execution, show a refreshed preview and require renewed confirmation. Do not silently approve a smaller/different subset. Financial effects execute per existing payout with independent durable outcomes; do not promise cross-row rollback after PayPal has accepted a send.
- Distinguish **requested/reserved gross**, **eligible selected gross**, **estimated fee**, **estimated net to recipients**, **actual fee/net**, **held** and **not yet earned**. Use integer minor units and explicit currency. Never total USD and EUR as one monetary amount; never call reserved member funds team revenue or PayPal spendable balance. A fresh balance check is needed for cumulative sends/floor, not the same cached balance reused for every row.

### 5.5 Stale, repeated and uncertain work

Persist proposal id, exact canonical inputs/effects, subject/material versions, pinned rulebook ids, operational policy revision, required confirmer set, expiry and idempotency identity. Each external effect has an owned durable intent/claim; the model's run id alone is not provider idempotency. Consume human confirmation and record the business decision atomically. Existing action logs are best effort ([perform.ts, lines 100–130](../../packages/api/src/actions/perform.ts#L100)); therefore they cannot be the only durable proof of a new delegated financial decision.

At final execution or side-effect claim, recheck authority and material guards under the appropriate domain lock. Use current code's claim/reconciliation discipline; do not hold a database transaction over a model/network call. Current payout claim can discover a newly opened dispute and preserves send identity on uncertain retry ([payouts.ts, lines 579–639](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/payouts.ts#L579)). No implementation can retract an already dispatched provider request; revocation stops new dispatches, while a possibly sent operation must still be reconciled to avoid a duplicate or lost liability.

Duplicate event → same decision episode/preparation key; repeated confirmation → prior outcome or current conflict without new money; model timeout → visible manual path; read retry → bounded; unknown provider response → reconciliation with original identity, not a fresh send. A definitive failure and a deliberately authorized new attempt are different from an uncertain replay. Retry ceilings and manual escalation come from existing domain/provider windows, not model confidence. Failures updating a Call never roll back a payment; its projection rebuilds from records. Exhausted model budget stops discretionary model work, not refunds owed, deterministic notices, provider reconciliation or access checks.

The SDK/provider seam remains a validation task: intended Muse Spark through Claude Agent SDK, constrained tool transport, cancellation, persistence and resume must be exercised before asserting compatibility. The calls design explicitly calls its deployment a proposal ([lines 111–115, 170–175](2026-10-27-calls-and-agent-design.md#L111)). No new runtime selection or official deadline research is made here.

### 6.2 Calls triage should answer a person's actual job

Compute `requiredDecisions` separately from `eligibleActions`. A Call can contain more than one required decision, and people may see different triage for the same episode:

- **Needs you:** at least one unresolved required decision that this person currently can take, or an owned correction task they can perform. Ordinary payment/review waits are not warnings merely because they wait on someone else.
- **In progress:** outcome unresolved, but currently waits on another approver, customer, worker, timer, PayPal or an authorized in-flight operation. A failed model draft should preserve the person's available manual decision.
- **Done:** the declared outcome has been reached—e.g. quote sent for that intake episode, payout settled, or explicit terminal rejection/failure. “Done” needs an outcome label; a failed payout is not Paid.

After the first of two approvals, the first approver sees waiting while the second sees Needs you. If nobody eligible remains, create an admin configuration exception rather than offering an unauthorized approval. A human may dismiss/close a question, not dismiss an unsettled business obligation. Later events can create a new decision episode on the same record; a new delivery review is not a second project lifecycle. Store event dedupe and episode/outcome identity independently of handle names. Per-user read markers are orthogonal to triage.

Counts say what they count: “1 Call needs you,” “2 payouts awaiting your approval,” “7 of 14 payouts shown.” A two-payout batch is one Call, not two contradictory sidebar totals. Shared Calls, section tables and account summaries use the same authorized projection timestamp/filter and expose stale/error state when refresh fails.

### 7.2 Observed gaps worth resolving before expanding agent access

These are source-level findings, not exploitation tests or a blanket audit. Fix/verify the narrow contracts; do not declare the whole product insecure.

1. **Rival quote detail is not filtered at the team project response.** The current design requires freelancers see quote count and only their own detail ([contextual reference, lines 84–90, 268](2026-10-18-contextual-dash-ontology-design.md#L84)). `getTeamProjectView` loads all quotes and returns `quotes` unchanged ([project-views.ts, lines 113–143](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/project-views.ts#L113)); the repository query filters only by project ([projects repository, lines 55–61](../../packages/db/src/repositories/projects.ts#L55)). This response cannot be reused as a worker-agent context. Confirm all adapter paths and apply server field filtering; examine quote-bearing conversation events as well. Client and staff comparison remains permitted.
2. **Abandoned project conversation closure drifts.** `abandoned` is a terminal project status, but conversation `CLOSED` omits it ([state-machine.ts, lines 1–12, 114](../../packages/core/src/projects/state-machine.ts#L1), [access.ts, lines 25–54](../../packages/core/src/conversations/access.ts#L25)). The observed evaluator can allow messages on an abandoned project for otherwise eligible participants. Resolve the intended terminal behavior explicitly and cover its real path; do not duplicate another terminal-status list in Calls.
3. **Open-request deadline display remains fixed at 48h.** `listOpenRequests` computes `respondBy` from a constant ([project-views.ts, lines 175–197](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/project-views.ts#L175)), while B1 supports pinned durations and staging exercised two hours ([staging, lines 26–30](plans/2026-10-27-b1-staging-walkthrough.md#L26)). Derive displayed time from the same pinned schedule facts before agent deadline explanations.
4. **Role checks are not all at the final side-effect claim.** The payout router checks admin permission, then passes `deciderId`; approval counts stored ids ([router, lines 184–190](../../packages/api/src/routers/payouts.ts#L184), [payouts.ts, lines 524–547](../../packages/api/src/use-cases/payouts.ts#L524)). The inspected claim guards destination/credentials/dispute, not current validity of earlier approvers. Add the proposed live-authority contract for agent-enabled paths; do not infer it from today's role gate or historic approval count.
5. **Terms acceptance in model-callable request remains unresolved.** Recommend keep request model-callable but record it as an uncommitted inquiry; obtain exact pinned working-terms acceptance at the human quote-accept card/form. Keep web request checkbox evidence where real, and distinguish the two acceptance moments. This is a proposed consent change requiring a deliberate spec/product decision, not a fix already made ([BACKLOG, line 19](../BACKLOG.md#L19), [B1 plan, lines 552–559](plans/2026-10-22-b1-operating-model.md#L552)).

## 8. Decision log for the parent

### Recommended defaults that can be adopted in the design now

- One agent, capability/relationship-specific views; no distinct agency/studio/customer state machines.
- Existing organization/workspace and seller identities remain distinct; subscriptions control supported capabilities, not record authority.
- Personal/My purchases as private projections over seller-owned records; account is the authorized summary root. No auto-claim of guest orders by email.
- Handles route; roles/grants authorize; fixed roles first, editable permission bundles later. Existing workspace admin inheritance stays local and explicit.

- Current access and hard gates override stale context; pinned project terms survive mode changes. Operational policy separately versioned, with legacy schedules surfaced rather than silently altered.
- Human-confirmed intake and payout decisions first; real deterministic payout automation can be demonstrated with correct attribution. No unsupported Pay now or arbitrary Friday schedule.
- Private mixed-context questions; homogeneous shared Call audiences; no automatic publication of agent chat into customer messages.
