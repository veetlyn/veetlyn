## 1. Your Goal: Hackathon Problem Statements

Your project must build towards one of the following problem statements:

**Build for Your World** - Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that wasn't before.

**Push the Frontier** - Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show us what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.

Your project must incorporate Meta's models. Use the [Muse Spark series](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-spark), [Muse Wonder](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-wonder), [Muse Image](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-image) [Muse Voice Transcribe](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-voice-transcribe) and [SAM 3.1](https://dev.meta.ai/models/sam-3-1) through [Meta Model API](https://dev.meta.ai/products/meta-model-api), run Meta open weights models [Muse Glimmer](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-glimmer) and [Muse Spark Open](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-spark-open), and code faster with [Muse Code](https://dev.meta.ai/lp/muse-code) coding agent.

## 4. Hackathon Rules

- **Open source:** Repositories **must be public**.
- **Team size:** A **maximum of four** participants per team. Solo participants are allowed.
- **Demo requirements:** Your demo **must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built during the Hackathon**. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at Judges sole discretion.
- **New work only:** What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in your submissions. Startups may build on existing products or codebases, but you must clearly identify what was newly developed during the Hackathon.
- **Banned projects:** Projects will be **disqualified** if they: violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, use code, data, or assets you do not have the rights to.

### Sample Anti-Projects

The following list contains project examples that are generic (seen across many hackathons) or violate platform policies. This is not an exhaustive list, but please make sure to avoid building: Any project using AI to generate and give medical advice e.g. AI Mental Health Advisor, skin blemish detector, etc.; Basic RAG Applications; Simple Image Analyzers; simple “AI for Education” Chatbots; AI Job Application Screener; AI Nutrition Coach; Personality Analyzers; Sports analyzers or coaches; Anything political or involving war

## 8. Judging Process

Judging Process will happen in three stages:

**Stage One:** The first stage will determine via pass/fail whether the ideas meet a baseline level of viability, in that the Project reasonably fits the theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta's open-weight models to build the project in the Hackathon.

**Stage Two:** All Submissions that pass Stage One will be evaluated in Stage Two based on the following criteria using a scale from 0-10:

**Judging Rubric**

| Category (Weight) | Description |
|---|---|
| **Impact (30%)** | What's the real-world potential here? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit into one of the problem statements listed above? |
| **Use of Meta AI Tooling (25%)** | How creatively did this team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised even us? |
| **Technical Depth & Execution (20%)** | Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does this feel like something that was thought through with real craft. |
| **Demo (25%)** | Is this a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch? |

**Stage Three:** The top 5 scoring projects per track move on to a final round of judging, where final round Judges will watch the video demos live and rank them using the same rubric.
