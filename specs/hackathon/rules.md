# Official rules (summary)

This is a working summary of the PayPal AI Hackathon Official Rules, fetched 2026-10-03. Where wording matters, it is quoted or paraphrased closely. The legal text at <https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules> is authoritative. Submitting an entry means agreeing to the rules as a contract with PayPal and Devpost.

## Eligibility

**Who can enter:**

- Individuals at or above the age of majority where they live.
- Teams of such individuals.
- Organizations that exist at the time of entry, including companies, nonprofits, LLCs, and partnerships.

**Teams and individual entries:**

- A person may join several teams or organizations, and may also enter as an individual.
- A team or organization must appoint one eligible **Representative**, who submits the entry and receives any prize.

**Who cannot enter:**

- Residents or organizations of places where US or local law prohibits taking part. These include **Brazil, Quebec, Russia, Crimea, Cuba, Iran, North Korea**, and any country under comprehensive OFAC sanctions.
- PayPal, Devpost, and any other organization involved in running or promoting the hackathon. This extends to their employees, representatives, agents, and immediate family or household members.
- Judges, and any company or person that employs a judge.
- Parents, subsidiaries, and affiliates of any of the above.
- Anyone whose participation creates a real or apparent conflict of interest, at the sponsor's discretion.

## 4. How to enter

1. Register on the hackathon site with the **Join hackathon** button. You need a free Devpost account.
2. Get access to the PayPal sandbox with an existing PayPal account, or create one at <https://developer.paypal.com/>.
3. Build the project, then fill in every required field on the **Enter a Submission** page before the deadline.

### Project requirements

- **What to build.** Build a new application, or update an existing one, that integrates the **PayPal developer platform (using the free sandbox)** with **an AI tool, model, or platform of your choice**.
  - "PayPal" covers all PayPal services. Examples are Venmo, Braintree, Xoom, Honey, Zettle, Paidy, Hyperwallet, Simility, and Swift Financial.
  - Sponsor AI tools are optional. Any AI tool works, as long as the **PayPal integration is central** to the project.
  - The sponsor tools are AG Grid, APIMatic, Astropods, Bryntum, Channel3, Elastic, KERNEL, Postman, Render, and Zapier. See [`sponsors.md`](sponsors.md).
- **Functionality.** The project installs and runs consistently on its intended platform. It works as shown in the video and as described in the text.
- **New or existing project.** The project is either new, or **significantly updated after Oct 1, 2026**. For an existing project, explain what changed. PayPal and Devpost decide what counts as significant.
- **Third-party integrations.** You must be authorized to use any third-party SDK, API, or data, under that provider's terms.

### Submission requirements

- **Text description** of the features and functionality.
- **Functional demo.** Judges must be able to run or use a working build, in one of two ways:
  - clear, complete setup and run instructions in the repo, or
  - a hosted demo URL.

  Public hosting is not required. A mockup, static prototype, or non-functional demo does not qualify.
- **Public GitHub repository:**
  - It contains all the source, assets, and instructions needed to run the project.
  - It is open source, with a license file that GitHub detects and shows in the About section.
- **Demo video:**
  - It should run **under 3 minutes**. Judges do not have to watch past 3 minutes.
  - It shows the project running on the device it was built for.
  - It is **public on YouTube**, with the link on the submission form.
  - It contains no third-party trademarks, copyrighted music, or other copyrighted material without permission.
- **Tools.** The overview page also asks which tools you used and how you used them.

### Other entry conditions

- **Multiple submissions.** You may submit more than one, but each must be substantially different from the others.
- **Testing access:**
  - Provide a website, a working demo, or a test build.
  - For a private site, include login credentials.
  - Include any test accounts, API keys, or sandbox logins that judges need, or steps for getting access.
  - Keep the project **free and unrestricted** for PayPal, Devpost, and the judges until judging ends.
  - Judges may score from the description, images, and video alone.
- **Special hardware.** If the project runs on uncommon hardware, meaning anything other than a phone, tablet, or computer, judges may require physical access to it.
- **Language.** All materials are in English, or come with English translations of the video, description, and testing instructions.
- **Ownership and IP:**
  - The work must be original, owned solely by the entrant, and must not infringe anyone's rights.
  - Contracted technical help is allowed, as long as the entrant owns the result and the ideas are theirs.
  - Open source dependencies are allowed if their licenses are respected. The project must also build on and extend them.
- **No sponsor funding.** The project must not have been developed with funding, a contract, a commercial license, or other preferential support from PayPal or Devpost.

## 5. Changes to a submission

- Drafts can be saved and edited until the deadline.
- After the deadline the submission is frozen. Your Devpost portfolio project can still be updated.
- PayPal and Devpost may allow narrow fixes after the deadline, only to remove infringing, personal, or inappropriate material.

## 6. Judging

PayPal and Devpost choose the judging methods. These may include expert panels, peer review, **automated AI-driven analysis**, or a mix. The listed judges may change, and there may be several rounds.

- **Stage one, pass or fail.** Does the project reasonably fit the theme and reasonably use the required APIs or SDKs?
- **Stage two, five equally weighted criteria:**
  1. **Technological implementation.** Thorough, skillful use of the PayPal developer platform and the AI tools. Genuine effort and a working, non-trivial implementation.
  2. **Design.** A complete, coherent product experience, not just a technical proof of concept.
  3. **Potential impact.** A credible, specific case for solving a real problem for a real audience, which the demo actually shows.
  4. **Innovation and idea.** Creative, novel, and different from existing concepts.
  5. **Presentation.** The video shows the project working end to end. The pitch says what problem it solves, for whom, and why it matters, and it is easy to follow.

**Tie-break.** Ties are broken by the higher score on criterion 1, then 2, and so on. If submissions are tied on every criterion, the judges vote.

## 7. Intellectual property

- Entrants keep ownership of their submissions. PayPal gets a non-exclusive license to use a submission for judging.
- PayPal and Devpost may promote the submission and use the names, likenesses, voices, and images of contributors. This applies during the hackathon and for **3 years** after it.
- The entrant warrants that the submission contains no unlicensed third-party content and no malware.

## 9 to 15. Legal terms (summary)

- **§9, entry conditions and release.** There is no special or fiduciary relationship with PayPal or Devpost. Decisions by PayPal, Devpost, and the judges are final. Entrants indemnify PayPal, Devpost, and their partners, who are not liable for technical failures or for late or lost submissions.
- **§10, publicity.** Entrants consent to promotional use of the submission and of their name, likeness, voice, opinions, hometown, and country, worldwide and without payment.
- **§11, general conditions:**
  - PayPal and Devpost may cancel, suspend, or modify the hackathon, and may disqualify anyone for tampering or misconduct.
  - **The rules prevail over other hackathon materials.** The rules may be amended by posting the change on the site.
  - Requests to clarify an ambiguous rule must be made in writing before the deadline.
- **§12, limitation of liability.** Entrants release PayPal, Devpost, and their partners, except where the law forbids such limits.
- **§13, disputes.** Disputes go to individual binding arbitration under the AAA rules, with no class actions. The Federal Arbitration Act applies, and New York law governs. Punitive and consequential damages are waived.
- **§14, additional terms.** The Devpost Terms of Service apply (<https://info.devpost.com/terms>). The rules win any conflict.
- **§15, personal information.** The Devpost Privacy Policy applies (<https://info.devpost.com/privacy>). Contact: support@devpost.com.
